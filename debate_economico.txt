2. GE Implications: The Endogenous Feedback of r
The core of the GE problem is how the real interest rate (r) reacts to τ.
The r-τ Compression: As τ increases, the efficiency of money increases. In a standard Taylor Rule environment, if V rises due to technology, the central bank should see an inflationary spike (PY rising) and raise r to cool the economy.
The Productivity Paradox: However, if τ also lowers the "Transaction Cost" (the Z good in the previous analysis), it acts as a massive supply-side shock. This puts downward pressure on P. 3. Critiquing the Friction Function ψ(τ,γ)
The previous cost function minTC=i⋅(P/M)+ψ(τ,γ)⋅(M/PY) assumes that τ and γ are independent variables. This is a general equilibrium flaw. In a GE state, γ (credibility) is an emergent property of τ (technology).
The Filter Critique: We cannot use a standard Hodrick-Prescott (HP) filter on FRED M2 velocity data anymore. The "leakage" of liquidity into non-bank financial intermediaries (shadow banking) means FRED data captures a shrinking slice of total transactions.
The BIS Relevance: BIS CPMI (Red Book) data is the only way to observe the "Latent Velocity." We must compare the Gross Settlement Volume (the "pipes") to the Net Monetary Base. If settlement volume/MB ratio is exploding while V in FRED is flat, the friction ψ is not decreasing for the household—it is decreasing for the financial ledger, creating a Velocity Gap.
4. Advancing the Theory: The "Velocity-Induced Liquidity Trap"
I propose that at extremely high levels of τ, we enter a new type of liquidity trap. When V is nearly infinite (instantaneous settlement), the central bank loses control over the price level because the "Quantity of Money" (M) approaches zero in the limit, while V approaches infinity. The equation PY=MV becomes mathematically indeterminate (0⋅∞).
The GE Equilibrium Shift: We move from a regime of Price Stability to a regime of Collateral Stability. The interest rate r ceases to be the price of money and becomes the price of trust (γ). If γ is high, r can be low; if γ fails, r must go to infinity to compensate for the "Verification Tax," regardless of the money supply.
